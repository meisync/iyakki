# IYAKKI audio mixer

Status: core, one entry point (`audiosink.rs`) and the Settings -> Sound switch implemented and tested (`crates/iyakki-agent/src/mixer/`), **off by default** (`sound.mixer`), not yet run on a real T2 Mac.

## Why

On the MacBookPro16,2 (`AppleT2x4`, one playback subdevice, S24_LE, 4 channels, 48 kHz) ALSA `dmix` uses about 90 % of a core. So the T2 plan is `plug > softvol > plughw`: one program at a time, and the SDK answers `Busy` ([`SDK-API.md`](SDK-API.md), owner's answer 1). The owner asked for an in-house mixer instead of dmix.

## Process model

One mixer thread inside the agent (`Services`, process-wide instance `mixer::global()`, started on first use). It is the only writer to the card: it feeds `aplay -q -D <IYAKKI output> -t raw -f S16_LE -r 48000 -c 2 -` through a pipe (the same mechanism the radio and the cues use), so the existing `iyakki` PCM (`plug > softvol > plughw`) still carries the master volume and the Mute switch exactly once. No new daemon, no unix socket yet.

* Sources (`Mixer::open_source(kind, rate, channels) -> SourceWriter`) write plain interleaved PCM at any rate (8-192 kHz) and 1-8 channels into a 500 ms ring. The mixer converts rate and channel layout. `write_i16_wait` blocks when the ring is full, which paces a decoder to the speaker.
* **Decoders are sources.** The in-house MP3 decoder, and later the optional user-downloaded ffmpeg helper (`iyakki-audiodec`, a separate process writing s16le PCM plus rate and channels on stdout), feed a source with `SourceWriter::feed_reader(reader)` (a thread that reads a pipe, waits for room and finishes the source at end of data). The mixer never depends on ffmpeg.
* The engine (`engine.rs`) is pure: `mix_period` takes 20 ms (960 frames) from each source, resamples (`resample.rs`, windowed sinc, 16 zero crossings, Hann, tabulated), maps channels (mono to all, stereo to N like `softvol::fan_route`, N to stereo folds even/odd), applies per-source gain, ducking and master gain with linear ramps across the period, adds in f32 and saturates to 16-bit (`clamp`, counted as `clipped`).
* Pacing (changed 2026-10-25 after breaks heard on the T2 with radio and reading aloud): the thread keeps **15 periods (300 ms) of mixed audio written ahead of the card** (`MixerCfg.lead_periods`, was 2 = 40 ms). The card's timeline is modelled as `anchor + written`; the thread sleeps only while it is more than the lead ahead, so a thread that was not scheduled for up to ~280 ms mixes the missed periods back to back and the card never notices. When the card does run dry (the model says it is behind) that is counted as a **late period** (`Stats.sink_late`) and the model re-anchors. When the sink opens, the whole lead is written first as silence (pre-roll), so the card never starts empty and sources holding less than the lead are not drained in a burst. The pipe to aplay is grown to 256 KB (`F_SETPIPE_SZ`) so the lead fits and a write blocks only when the card is really behind; aplay gets `--buffer-time=500000 --period-time=50000` (a 50 ms period is inside the T2 driver's 256 to 4096 frames); if the device refuses them the sink retries with aplay's defaults, the known-good path. The device chain (`plug > softvol > plughw`) is not changed. Clock drift against the card (tens of ppm) is absorbed by the blocking pipe; a card that is faster than the clock would slowly eat the lead (not measured on the T2).
* Heard-position model (2026-10-25): the card does not play before its buffer is full, so a sample mixed at time `t` is heard at `t` plus what the sink holds beyond the lead. `Sink::latency_ms()` says it (`AplaySink`: 500 ms tuned, 347 ms with aplay's defaults, 0 for fakes) and the mixer adds `max(0, latency - lead)` (200 ms) to the end time of every period, which is what `Source::played_ms` subtracts. This replaces the fixed `CARD_BUFFER_MS = 350` subtraction in `audiosink.rs`, which made the highlight of TALES lag. The model is derived, not measured on the T2: Settings -> Sound -> Advanced -> Highlight timing offset (`audiosink::highlight_offset_ms`) tunes the rest, and each read sentence logs its heard and clock numbers.
* Latency: ducking and new sources reach the card after about 300 ms (lead) plus the ALSA buffer. Word highlighting therefore must not use wall time: `SourceWriter::played_ms()` / `Output::played_ms()` give the heard position (mixed time minus what is still in flight to the speaker; it stands still while a source is starved) and `WordClock::follow` uses it.
* Jitter buffers (per kind, `EngineCfg`): radio and media start mixing after **800 ms** queued and, after running dry, resume at **400 ms**; speech 250 ms / 250 ms; notifications and alarms 40 ms. Rings hold 6 s (radio, media), 2 s (speech), 500 ms (others), so a decoder is paced (blocked, not polling) by the speaker. A source below its level is silent and does not hold the others up, and it does not open the card by itself (no open/close churn). A source that runs dry fades out over 5 ms and fades in again on resume (no crackle). Counted separately: **source underruns** (`Stats.underruns`, `Stats.starved` per kind) versus **late periods** (`Stats.sink_late`).
* Scheduling: the thread asks for `nice -10` and `SCHED_RR` priority 10 (failure is ignored; the result is shown as `Stats.priority`). The ring locks are held for at most ~90 ms of audio per write, no lock shared with the UI is held while mixing.

## CPU budget

Target under 3 % of one core while mixing four sources. The cost is the resampler (about 3 M multiply-adds per second for a 44.1 kHz stereo source) plus one add per sample. Mixing time is measured per period and published as `Stats.cpu_percent`. A test mixes four sources for 10 s of audio and bounds the time (5 % of real time release, 30 % debug; in a debug build it measured about 16 %, a release number has not been taken yet).

## Idle, silence and shutdown

No data from any source for `idle_ms` (1.5 s): the thread closes the pipe (aplay drains and exits), the card is released and can sleep, and other programs may open it. With no source at all the thread sleeps (about 4 wake-ups per second). The next source reopens the card; audio waits in its ring meanwhile, nothing is lost. A source that sends digital silence keeps the card open (documented, not special-cased). The T2 shutdown behaviour of `quiet.rs` is unchanged: it finds the `aplay` holder like any other and ends it, and the softvol goes to 0 first.

## Priorities and ducking

alarm > speech > notification > media > radio. While a source is sounding (audible within the last 300 ms hang, so speech does not pump between sentences), every source of LOWER priority is brought to a factor: alarm 0.10 (-20 dB), speech 0.25 (-12 dB), notification 0.40 (-8 dB); media and radio do not duck. Attack 30 ms, release 400 ms, both linear and interpolated per sample. A paused source does not duck. Per-source gain (0-4) is smoothed over a period; master gain is too.

## Opening, `Busy` and exclusive

| situation | answer |
|---|---|
| mixer off (`sound.mixer = off`, default) | `Disabled`: the caller keeps its old path; the SDK `audio.play` / `speech.say` say `Busy` as before |
| mixer on | a source is always accepted (no more `Busy` for a second program) unless: the card cannot be opened (`Busy{holder}` for 2 s, e.g. a program outside the mixer holds it), an exclusive source is open (only alarms pass), or 16 sources are open (a more important source evicts the oldest least important one; an equal one gets `Busy`) |
| exclusive source (`open_source_with(.., exclusive = true)`) | refused unless nothing else plays; others get `Busy{holder}`; at unity gain, same rate and channels, the 16-bit samples pass through unchanged |

Who may open: UI code, radio, cues, speech and media players are IYAKKI's own. SDK pods need the `audio` (or `speech`) capability from `app.json`, checked by the host before `open_audio_source`; per-pod kind is `media` (`audio.play` channel `music`), `notification` (`cue`) or `speech`. Pods can never open `alarm`.

## Volume and mute

The softvol control `IYAKKI` (or the hardware control) stays the master: the mixer plays into the same `iyakki` PCM. Where the selector has no softvol (players scale themselves), the mixer follows `Selector::gain()` as its master factor each period (0 when muted).

## Sound doctor and Devices

`Services::mixer_stats()` / `mixer::Mixer::stats()`: running, sink open, source count, CPU percent, underruns (total and by kind), late periods, queued ms of the nearest-to-dry stream, ms ahead of the card, the mixer thread's own CPU percent, thread priority, sink buffer sizes, clipped samples, ducking, last problem; `Stats::line()` is the short text and `Stats::detail()` the longer one that Settings -> Sound shows (`... % of a core · N underrun(s) from sources (radio, media, speech, other) · N late period(s) · N ms buffered, N ms ahead · mixer thread N% CPU · priority ...`). Many late periods with no source underruns: the mixer thread is not being scheduled. Many source underruns with no late periods: the decoder or network is too slow. `audioout::Status.sharing` says `OneAtATime`, `Dmix` or `IyakkiMixer` (shown as "IYAKKI mixer" in the summary). The browser-silent doctor (`sounddoctor.rs`) is unchanged: it still reads `/proc/asound` and will see the mixer's `aplay` as a holder of the card.

## Setting

`sound.mixer` (`SoundSettings.mixer`, default false). `audioout::sharing_for(plan, mixer_on)`: dmix plans stay dmix; exclusive plans (T2 fixed-period, hardware-volume cards) become `IyakkiMixer`. The ALSA file is unchanged (`Plan::Soft`), so turning the setting off needs no rewrite. The Settings -> Sound switch is described below.

## One entry point: `audiosink.rs`

Apps do not know about the mixer (owner, 2026-10-24: "why does every app need to know"). The agent has one place that asks for the speaker:

* `audiosink::open_output(kind, rate, channels, label, play_cmd) -> Output` (streaming PCM; `Services::open_output` fills in the play command) and `audiosink::play_wav(kind, label, wav, play_cmd) -> Output` (a WAV), `audiosink::play_wav_blocking(..)` (a short cue, returns when played).
* `Output` has `write(&[i16])`, `set_volume(pct)`, `finish(max, keep_waiting)`, `stop()`, `is_playing()`, `failure()`.
* Mixer on (`sound.mixer`): a mixer source with the kind's priority; the master volume and mute are the mixer's, the output's own volume is the source gain. If the mixer cannot take the sound (a program outside it holds the card) the play-command path below is used.
* Mixer off: exactly the old behaviour, the play command of Settings -> Voice started with a pipe, the output and softvol plan of Settings -> Sound, `gain()`, one program at a time.
* `audiosink::mixing()` tells the SDK whether programs share the speaker: `speech.say` returns no `Busy` while it is true, priority and ducking decide; with it off the old `Busy` rules hold (`SDK-API.md`). `audio.play` has no real backend yet.
* A test (`audiosink_tests::only_the_entry_point_knows_about_the_mixer_setting`) fails if any agent file other than `audiosink.rs`, `audioout.rs` (which applies the setting) and `mixer/` reads `mixer::enabled`, `global_if_enabled`, `prefs.mixer` or `open_audio_source`.

Kinds: radio (`RadioPlayer`, kind Radio), reading aloud and SDK speech (`voice::Player::start`, kind Speech; `Player::start_as` for another kind), system sound cues (`syssounds::SystemOut`, kind Notification), audio files the built-in media path plays through `RadioPlayer` (`media.rs`, kind Radio today). Alarm is for future alarms; nothing sounds one yet.

Not on the entry point: the media player's ffmpeg/aplay pipeline (`player.rs`, an external process that writes to `aplay` itself), host speech engines that speak by themselves (espeak-ng, speech-dispatcher: they open the speaker themselves, `Player::from_speech`), the Settings test sounds and speaker tests (they test a chosen device on purpose), `quiet.rs`, and the browser (which has the pipe route above, off by default).

## Settings -> Sound switch

Settings -> Sound has the row **Mix sounds from several apps (experimental)** (`M`, button Mix sounds, `Enter` on the row; administrators only, audited as `settings.sound.mixer`), saved in `SoundSettings.mixer` through `update_sound`, which calls `audioout::apply_prefs` and so `mixer::set_enabled`. Its explanation says the browser's sound is mixed only with the separate row below (and that, without it, the browser asks before it takes the card from a player). While it is on, a status row shows running or idle, sources, CPU percent, underruns from sources, late periods, buffered ms and the mixer thread's CPU (`mixer::stats_if_enabled`). It applies from the next sound: a radio that already plays keeps its old player until its next restart.

* **Browser audio: an experimental route and a dialog, neither proven on hardware.** WPE/GStreamer's `alsasink` opens ALSA directly (`iyakkiweb` PCM from `softvol::web_pcm`) and holds the card while it plays, so with the mixer open the two compete. There is no PulseAudio/PipeWire here, an ALSA `ioplug` plugin would be C code (out of scope), and `snd-aloop` is not in the kernel fragments (a loopback card needs a kernel change). Two things were built instead:
  1. **Route through a pipe (`webmix.rs`, candidate 2 of the earlier plan).** Settings -> Sound has a second row, **Browser sound through the mixer (experimental)** (`W`, `Enter`; administrators only; audited as `settings.sound.web_mix`; `SoundSettings.web_mix`, default off, no effect while the mixer is off). With both on, `webengine::helper_env` gives the web engine `ALSA_CONFIG_PATH` pointing at `web-alsa-mix.conf` in the data folder: ALSA's own file, then `pcm.iyakkiwebmixfile { type file; slave.pcm "null"; file "<data>/web-sound.fifo"; format raw }`, `pcm.iyakkiwebmix { type plug; slave { pcm iyakkiwebmixfile; format S16_LE; rate 48000; channels 2 } }` and `pcm.!default iyakkiwebmix`. Before the engine can open it, `webmix::ensure` makes the named pipe (mode 0600) and starts the **tap** thread, which holds the pipe open read-write (so the engine's open never blocks and the pipe never reports end of file), polls it, turns the bytes into samples and writes them to a mixer source of kind Media labelled "Browser" (`write_i16_wait`, so a full ring blocks the tap, the pipe fills, and the engine's write blocks: the pipe is the clock). 400 ms without data finishes the source so the mixer can let the card sleep. If the mixer refuses the source (an outside program holds the card) the data is dropped at the pace of real time and the status row says so. The status row ("Browser sound status") is honest: off / does nothing while the mixer is off / applies the next time the web engine starts (untested on real hardware) / connected / carrying / refused. With the route active, `web_autoplay_env` is not applied (the card is shared, so autoplay need not be denied). **Not proven:** that the image's alsa-lib has the `file` plugin (it is part of alsa-lib's core PCM plugins, but the build was not inspected), that `alsasink` keeps a steady audio clock over a `null`-backed PCM (sound and picture may drift), the latency, and that the engine's user may open the pipe. Tested only with a fake pipe writer and a fake sink (`webmix.rs` tests). The media player (`player.rs`) is not covered.
  2. **A dialog when the card is in the way (`soundhold.rs`, `apps/soundask.rs`).** IYAKKI's own players announce themselves to `soundhold` (the radio from `Services`, TALES' reader from the TALES app, the browser while its page plays and is not routed through the mixer): a sentence while they hold the speaker ("Radio is playing X", "TALES is reading", "The browser is playing sound") and a clean stop. When the browser's page plays, no stream of the web engine runs after 1.2 s, and the card is held by such a player or by a program outside IYAKKI that owns a running playback stream (found by `sounddoctor::scan`; anything below the agent's own process and pid 1 are never offered), the browser shows a dialog "X. Stop it so the browser can play?" with **Stop it / Keep it / Cancel** (`y` or Enter, `n`, Esc). Stop it stops the own player by its own function (radio stop, reader stop) and waits up to 3 s for it to let go, or ends the outside program through `quiet::stop_owner` (only the owner of an open playback stream right now; never pid 1 or the agent; SIGTERM, then SIGKILL after 1 s; bounded by 1.5 s) on a thread, and then makes the page's media pause and play again (`RECONNECT_JS`) so its sink opens the freed card. Keep it leaves everything; the dialog does not come back while the page keeps playing. Cancel closes it and it may come back after a minute. Each answer is audited (`browser.sound.ask|stop|stopped|stopfailed|keep|cancel`) with only the kind (`own` or `outside`) and the name of the holder, never anything else. The reverse: starting a radio station while another own player holds the speaker (and, with the mixer on, only the browser counts, since it is not in the mixer) shows the same dialog (`radio.sound.*`); Stop it on the browser asks the shell loop to pause the page's media (`PAUSE_JS`). Tested with fake kernel trees, fake signals and fake players; not seen on the Mac.
