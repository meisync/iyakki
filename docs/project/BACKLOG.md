## Scope of v1.0.0 (owner decision, 2026-10-24)

**Every item in this file is part of v1.0.0 unless the owner moves it out.** Pre-releases (alpha.2, beta, release candidates, see `RELEASE-READINESS.md`) still happen on the way; they are checkpoints, not a smaller scope.

### The release gate: v1.0.0 does not ship until all of this is true

1. **No known security issue.** Every item of the security review is closed or accepted in writing by the owner; no open high or medium finding; an outside review is done before the release candidate; dependencies, images and packages are signed and listed in the SBOM; secrets are never in logs, images or exports; the default settings are the safe ones.
2. **No strange or untrustworthy warnings in normal use.** On every supported machine a normal session shows no false alarms (for example the 'drive was disconnected' banner on a healthy NVMe), no scary banners for things that are fine, no certificate or signature warnings for IYAKKI's own services, packages or images, no kernel `WARN`/`ERROR` lines in the boot and session logs that we have not explained and either fixed or filtered with a stated reason, no compiler warnings (0 since 2026-10-26, held by `./run.sh warnings`), and no 'unsigned', 'dev' or 'experimental' marks left on shipped parts. A warning that stays must be true, useful and actionable.
3. **Everything fixed with testing.** Each item's definition of done (below) is met. The whole workspace test run is green on a clean checkout with no environment excuses (the /tmp quota, flaky tests triaged and fixed or removed); there is a working CI that runs it.
4. **Hardware matrix passed.** The owner's checklist is run on every machine on the supported list (at least the 2020 MacBook Pro T2, an AMD PC, one Intel PC, the older Mac mini): install from the image to internal and USB disks, boot, sound, Wi-Fi, camera, Touch Bar keys, browser video, shutdown, upgrade from the previous release, recovery.
5. **Upgrade and data safety proven.** An install made by the previous release upgrades without losing data (settings are versioned, fixtures from alpha.1 and beta are kept and tested); install and update tools never touch what they were not asked to touch.
6. **Docs true.** README, guides, KNOWN-ISSUES, HARDWARE and FEATURES say what is real.
7. **No licence issue and no proprietary-software issue (owner decision, 2026-10-24).** Every part that ships in an image, package or download has a licence that allows what we do with it, and we meet its conditions (notices reproduced, corresponding source offered for GPL parts, `legal-info/` complete). **Nothing proprietary or of doubtful redistribution is shipped**: the restricted firmware packs, Apple- or vendor-extracted files (Mac Wi-Fi/camera firmware), vendor CLIs (Claude Code, Copilot CLI, ffmpeg builds) and anything else we are not sure of are downloaded or extracted by the person at their click and never placed in an image or an export for others (`docs/RESTRICTED-FIRMWARE.md`). Model weights (LLM, TTS, speech) and fonts have their licences checked and shown. Codecs: no patent-encumbered codec ships unless the owner has decided it may (today H.264/AAC are removed from the image). An automated licence gate (deny list, allow list, SBOM, notice check; `iyakki-relcheck`) fails the build on a new unknown licence, and the owner's legal-opinion decisions (firmware, codecs, TTS weights) are made and recorded. **Open for the owner:** redistributable firmware blobs that the hardware needs (Intel, AMD, Mac Broadcom Wi-Fi in `linux-firmware`) are proprietary binaries with clear redistribution licences; they stay, listed with their licence texts, unless the owner wants a stricter 'no proprietary binary at all' line (which would remove Wi-Fi and graphics firmware from the default images and move them to the install-it-yourself route).

### Definition of done for every item

* A written requirement and, for anything with a design document, the owner's approval of the open decisions.
* Automated tests that fail without the change (unit, plus a headless/QEMU test where it touches boot, install or the screen); the narrow tests and the crate's whole lib tests green.
* A check on the real machine by the owner (or by an agent over SSH with the owner present) for anything hardware-facing, recorded with date and machine.
* A FEATURES.md row with real test names, docs updated, an audit/log line where it changes something, and no new compiler warning.
* Security review of the change when it touches secrets, boot, install, network, packages or approvals.

## RADIO: AAC and HLS (2026-10-24)

- Done: in-house HLS follower and MPEG-TS reader (`hls.rs`), MP3-in-HLS without any add-on, sniffing of MP3/AAC/HLS, AAC through a separate decoder program (`aacpipe.rs`), "Install audio add-on" action in RADIO, AAC/HLS stations flagged in `radio_stations.rs`.
- AAC works today with the vendor's `ffmpeg` (the Store's existing installer entry) and a fixed argument list. **Not built**: the planned helper `iyakki-audiodec` (a tiny program that loads the vendor's LGPL shared libraries and speaks the 10-byte-header protocol described at the top of `aacpipe.rs`), and a data-driven installer entry for those libraries (the owner supplies URL, sha256 and soname list; no vendor URL was invented). Nothing needs changing in the radio when the helper appears: it is looked up first.
- **Deferred**: an in-house AAC-LC decoder, for lack of the codebook tables of ISO/IEC 14496-3 (not reproducible from memory; no copying from other decoders allowed). HE-AAC (SBR/PS) stays unimplemented in IYAKKI (patent caution, legal opinion).
- Not played: HLS with fragmented-MP4 segments (the add-on could take them, the follower does not pass them on yet), encrypted HLS (AES-128, SAMPLE-AES), HLS titles from ID3.

## Performance: TALES frame time (measured on the T2 Mac, 2026-10-25; reworked, not yet measured on the Mac again)

* Before: 51-58 frames per 10 s while TALES read aloud, draw 54-80 ms, screen write 45 ms (full frame, 0 tile-only), slowest 150 ms; normal screens: 76-119 frames, draw ~17 ms, write ~2 ms.
* Causes found: `animating()` made every third heartbeat a whole redraw; `Gfx::clipped` copied the whole 20 MB frame on every call (TALES calls it several times a frame: about 55 of the 85 ms of a whole frame in the benchmark); `draw_pic` scaled the picture every frame; `glow_card` filled three huge rounded rectangles (8 ms); the screen write then compared and copied nearly every row because the card's glow changes the left and right edge of all of them.
* Done (2026-10-25): `Gfx::clipped` saves only what lies outside the clip in a reused buffer (all apps); `glow_card` draws its shapes only in a ring around the card's edge (the same picture, 8 ms to under 1 ms); the scaled picture is kept per picture, slot size and scale; the text layout is compared without copying the story; while the reader plays and nothing else moves, `StoryApp::redraw_kind` answers `Live` and `live_draw` repaints the changed lines, the player bar and (when the level moved it) the card glow, returning those rectangles; `Shell::take_live_rects` and `Framebuffer::present_rects` compare and copy only them; the whole-scene safety redraw is 1.5 s instead of 100 ms while an app paints live (`App::live_full_every_ms`). Benchmark (`cargo test --profile fast -p iyakki-shell --lib a_whole_frame_of_a_long_story`, 2880x1800, 3000 words, 1344x768 picture): whole frame about 85 ms before, about 16 ms after; a reading frame about 1 ms, 5-7 percent of the screen.
* Not done: (1) measure on the Mac with the frame log (expect `tile-only` to count most frames and the draw and write times to drop); (2) the multi-display driver (`backend_kms.rs` `present`) and the window and remote displays still copy whole frames and do not use partial frames (they do not call `live_update`); (3) present pipelining (draw frame N+1 while a present thread copies frame N) is not needed unless the Mac log still shows slow whole frames; (4) a sentence that moves to a new line scrolls the page, which is still a whole frame (about every few seconds); (5) below a scale of about 0.8 the glow of the text card is not repainted between whole frames (it would overlap the paragraph bar); (6) the pane (`textsel::Pane::line`) still copies each visible line and measures every character on each whole frame.
